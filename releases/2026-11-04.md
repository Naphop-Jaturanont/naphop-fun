## Learn by changing something

Take one litre of sealed air from the surface to 50 metres in an idealized ocean. Predict the volume at 10 m, distinguish one-third from one-quarter at 20 m, and bring the same air back to the surface. A depth slider, native preset buttons, conserved illustrative molecule dots, a correctly scaled spherical radius, and pressure/volume readouts expose the inverse relationship. No score or user data is collected. The homepage adds one learning card; all prior route entries remain.

Original visual artwork, copy and implementation. The prediction→experiment→explanation design tests whether confronting the “twice as deep, half again” intuition makes Boyle’s law easier to understand. This is a design hypothesis and local interaction check, not a measured learning study. No third-party code, illustration, branding or assets copied.

## Sources and model

Official pages read 4 Nov 2026:
- NOAA: pressure rises about one atmosphere per 10.06 m: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/pressure.html
- NASA Glenn: for fixed gas mass and constant temperature, pressure×volume is constant for an ideal gas: https://www1.grc.nasa.gov/beginners-guide-to-aeronautics/equation-of-state/
- NIST: standard atmosphere =101325 Pa: https://www.nist.gov/pml/special-publication-811/nist-guide-si-appendix-b-conversion-factors
- BIPM/JCGM: standard gravitational acceleration 9.80665 m/s²: https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.12.html
- NOAA WOD uses 1025 kg/m³ as an assumed constant seawater density, not a universal ocean constant: https://www.nodc.noaa.gov/OC5/WOD/wod18-notes.html

P=P₀+ρgh uses absolute pressure. V=V₀×P₀/P; sphere radius scales with cube root of volume ratio. At 10 m: 1.992037 atm, 50.1999% volume. At 20 m: 2.984074 atm, 33.5112%. Exact half-volume depth≈10.0803 m.

Assumptions displayed in both languages: ideal gas; equilibrium; constant temperature, gas amount, density and gravity; flexible enclosure without skin tension or elastic pressure; no leakage or dissolution. Not a real rubber balloon, rigid container or human body model. Educational only, not a dive-planning tool. Scene position is schematic, not a depth scale. Numbers show final equilibrium at the selected depth; brief motion is not real travel time.
