## Original experiment

A single marked dot rotates clockwise at 1 turn per second. Choose the camera's sampling rate, predict the shortest apparent path, capture six static frames, then scrub the unrecorded interval. Four optional discoveries compare stillness (1 sample/s), reverse motion (1.25 samples/s), a direction ambiguity (2 samples/s), and a forward match (4 samples/s). Wrong predictions still unlock the explanation. Exploration rates from 0.5 to 6 samples/s in 0.25 increments are supported.

The art, copy, code, and investigation sequence are original. No Neal.fun or third-party assets/code were reused. No Code Genius materials were consulted or included. The previous Birthday Room candidate was not reused.

Hypothesis: placing the actual position and a shortest-path interpretation on the same dial, then letting a learner uncover the missing interval, can make it clear that identical sampled evidence need not imply identical motion. This is a design hypothesis, not a measured learning outcome.

## Model

True rate f = +1 turn/s. Sampling rate f_s. Snapshot n occurs at t = n/f_s. Its angle is 360 × (n/f_s modulo 1) degrees clockwise from the top. The shortest-route displacement is the wrapped equivalent of f/f_s in (−0.5, +0.5) turns. A half-turn has two equally short directions and no unique signed rate.

At 1.25 samples/s: 0.8 true clockwise turns per picture but −0.2 shortest turns, equivalent to −0.25 turns/s. At 2 samples/s: +0.5 or −0.5 turns both fit. At 4 samples/s: +0.25 turns per picture, matching +1 turn/s. Full rotations are not distinguishable from instantaneous snapshots without an additional speed bound.

Assumptions are visible in Thai and English: constant speed, one unique marker, ideal instantaneous samples, no exposure blur, rolling shutter, noise, or repeated spokes. This is not a universal camera-FPS rule or a claim about what every viewer perceives. Displayed values are rounded; computations retain floating-point precision with near-zero angular wrapping normalized.
