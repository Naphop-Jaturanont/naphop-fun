## The experience

A fictional night-shift bridge office asks the visitor to stamp every bridge exactly once. Revisit islands, never reuse a bridge. Try the original city, add one parallel bridge, or add two. A route receipt, numbered stamps, map and inspector's notebook reveal how changing two degrees changes what is possible. Thai and English are available throughout. The theorem and bridge-puzzle tradition are established mathematics; the artwork, bilingual copy and implementation are newly created for this project. No third-party code or artwork was reused.

The learning hypothesis is that confronting a genuinely impossible map, then changing its connections, makes it easier to distinguish a bad route from an impossible task. This is a design hypothesis, not a measured learning outcome.

## Model and limits

Four connected islands A, B, C, D. Six base edges AB, BC, CD, DA, AC, BD form K4. All four degrees are 3, so no Euler trail exists. Extra edge 7 is another AB bridge: degrees 4,4,3,3; complete trails must begin at C or D and end at the other. Extra edge 8 is another CD bridge: all degrees 4; a complete closed route exists from every island. Parallel bridges are distinct. Lines crossing over water are not junctions.

This is a finite connected undirected multigraph, not the historical Königsberg map. Distance, timing and island revisits are unrestricted. The parity rule establishes existence, not success of any arbitrary route. Route success requires every edge exactly once. Plan changes and new-start selection clear history; undo restores the exact prior edge and island.
