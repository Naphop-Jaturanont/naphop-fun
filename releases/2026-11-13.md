## Learning and experience

Predict the probability before admitting 23 fictional visitors. Dates fill a bureau wall; matching dates are highlighted and a receipt counts all possible pairs. Reveal contrasts any matching pair with matching the first person, then try 10/23/40/70 people and 100 simulated rooms. Learn why 23 people provide 253 possible pairs and a 50.73% chance under the stated model, not the much smaller 5.86% chance of matching a particular first person.

Original bilingual copy, CSS illustration and implementation. No company assets. A fresh public interaction study of Neal.fun Password Game used invented text `hello`, then `hello7`: satisfying a rule progressively revealed the next question. Hypothesis: committing to an easy action before a reveal creates curiosity. Birthday Bureau applies prediction→experiment→explanation, not password rules, copied code or copied visual design.

## Sources and assumptions

Read again 13 November 2026: https://mathworld.wolfram.com/BirthdayProblem.html . MathWorld verifies independent uniform d-day model and 23-person probability 0.507297. Formula: 1 minus product (365-i)/365 for i=0..n-1. First visitor probability 1-(364/365)^(n-1), pair count n(n-1)/2, derived directly. All displayed rates are calculated, not demographic statistics.

Independent draws over 365 equally likely days, excluding leap day, comparing month/day only. Real birthdays need not be uniform. Fictional visitors; no real dates collected. One room's outcome is not proof of 0% or 100%; batch results vary. RNG uses rejection sampling to avoid modulo bias.
